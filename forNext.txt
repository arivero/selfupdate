I’d use them to make the model learn from the works while discussing them, then test what survives without reopening the text.

  For example, a session about Machado would work like this:

  1. A stanza is retrieved to answer a reader’s question. That immediate answer is retrieval-assisted—not evidence of memorization.
  2. While conversation continues, the passage-informed teacher supplies local corrections to the student’s layers. The vocabulary head stays
     frozen; gradients never cross layer boundaries.

  3. Completed updates become available to subsequent answers. Later, we ask a differently phrased question without retrieving the stanza.
  4. We repeat after discussing Cervantes, and again in a fresh session with only the saved learned weights. That tests both persistence and
     interference.

  The two proposals address different obstacles:

  - Learning on the student’s actual prefixes: can the correction help when the model chooses its own words, rather than being supplied the
    correct continuation?

  - Fast layer-local memory: can useful information be written quickly enough to help during the ongoing conversation?

  The hardest question is addressing: does learning under one question help answer another question about the same passage? A correction that
  only works when given its original cue is insufficient for expertise.

  We should measure three abilities separately:

   Ability           What would count
  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   Quotation         Accurate wording and attribution, without invented quotations.
  ────────────────  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   Knowledge         Correct answers about characters, events and connections, including unfamiliar question formulations.
  ────────────────  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   Interpretation    Coherent readings supported by the text, acknowledging ambiguity; assessed independently of the teaching model.

  Our current results support pursuing this, but don’t establish it: Qwen’s strongest gains were in cloze completion, not autonomous
  continuation. And one Machado poem plus four Quijote chapters cannot establish expertise across both authors.

  For practical use, I would retain retrieval for exact sourcing. Compare retrieval alone, learned memory alone, and their combination; fewer
  lookups are only an improvement if accuracy survives.
